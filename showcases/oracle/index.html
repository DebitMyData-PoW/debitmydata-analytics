Hashrate: how hard the network works

Hashrate is the computing power that secures the DebitMyData chain: how many SHA-256 hashes its miners try each second, all of them together. No miner reports it. It's read from the chain itself: each block's difficulty says how much work finding it took, and the time between blocks how fast that work was done.

More hashrate makes the chain harder to rewrite: to undo a payment, an attacker would need more computing power than everyone else combined.

02

Price: what one DMD costs to mine

DMD has no market price yet. Its reference price is the cost of the electricity it takes to mine one DMD: the chain's work over the last 4,032 blocks, at 20 J/TH and $0.06 per kWh, divided by the block reward. It never goes below DebitMyData's minimum rate of $0.001.

It follows the network: as hashrate grows, mining a coin costs more, and the reference price rises with it. It is not a market price, and nobody trades at it; the DebitMyData Agent Gateway charges with it.

03

Difficulty: set again at every block

Difficulty is how hard it is to find a block: how many hashes, on average, a miner tries before one is valid. DebitMyData sets it again at every block, from the last 60 blocks' times (LWMA), so blocks keep coming about every 150 seconds as miners arrive or leave.

Bitcoin adjusts its difficulty only every 2,016 blocks, about two weeks; DebitMyData follows a change in hashrate within the hour.
